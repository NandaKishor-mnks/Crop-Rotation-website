Helping Farmers Grow Healthy Crops Naturally — Gangavaram Village

Introduction to Crop Rotation

A simple and natural farming practice that helps maintain healthy soil and better crop production.

🌾 What is Crop Rotation?

Crop rotation is a farming practice in which different crops are grown in the same field in a planned sequence. Instead of growing the same crop continuously, farmers change the crop from one season to another.

This simple practice helps maintain soil fertility, reduce pest and disease problems, and support healthy crop growth.

Why is Crop Rotation Important?

🌱

Maintains Soil Fertility

Different crops use different nutrients, helping maintain a better nutrient balance.

🌍

Improves Soil Health

Different root systems help improve soil structure and support plant growth.

🐛

Reduces Pests

Changing crops can disturb pest life cycles and reduce pest problems.

🦠

Reduces Diseases

Crop rotation can reduce the build-up of crop diseases in the soil.

🌾

Improves Crop Yield

Healthy soil provides better conditions for crops to grow and produce well.

💧

Protects the Soil

Plant roots help hold the soil together and reduce soil erosion.

♻️

Supports Sustainable Farming

It helps farmers maintain productive land for future seasons.

💰

Reduces Fertilizer Dependence

Some crops can naturally improve the availability of important soil nutrients.

How Crop Rotation Works

Different groups of crops are grown one after another. Each group has different nutrient needs and effects on the soil.

🔄 The 4-Stage Crop Rotation Cycle

The crops are grown in a planned order. After completing the four stages, the cycle starts again.

The 4-Stage Rotation

Stage Crop Group Examples Main Role
1st 🌱 Legumes
Soil Builders 		Peas, Beans, Groundnut, Chickpea Helps improve nitrogen availability in the soil.
2nd 🥬 Leafy Crops
Heavy Feeders 		Spinach, Cabbage, Broccoli Uses available nutrients for healthy leaf growth.
3rd 🍅 Fruit Crops
Balanced Feeders 		Tomato, Cucumber, Pepper, Brinjal Uses nutrients for flowering and fruit development.
4th 🥕 Root Crops
Light Feeders 		Carrot, Radish, Beetroot Grows below the soil and supports soil structure.

🌱 1st – Legumes

Role: Soil Builders
Examples: Peas, Beans, Groundnut, Chickpea
Main Benefit: Improves nitrogen availability
Legumes help improve soil fertility and prepare the soil for the next crop.

🥬 2nd – Leafy Crops

Role: Heavy Feeders
Examples: Spinach, Cabbage, Broccoli
Main Benefit: Uses available nutrients
Leafy crops need a good supply of nutrients for healthy leaf growth.

🍅 3rd – Fruit Crops

Role: Balanced Feeders
Examples: Tomato, Cucumber, Pepper, Brinjal
Main Benefit: Supports fruit development
Fruit crops need balanced nutrients for healthy flowering and fruit production.

🥕 4th – Root Crops

Role: Light Feeders
Examples: Carrot, Radish, Beetroot
Main Benefit: Supports soil structure
Root crops grow below the soil and their roots help improve soil structure.

🌾 Why Follow the Rotation?

Following a planned crop rotation helps maintain soil fertility, reduce pests and diseases, improve soil health, and support better crop production. After the fourth stage, the cycle starts again with legumes.
